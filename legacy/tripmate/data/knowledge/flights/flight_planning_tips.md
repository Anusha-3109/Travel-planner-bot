# Flight planning tips

## Booking
- For long-haul flights, fares are often lowest when booked 2–6 months ahead. Peak periods such as summer holidays and late December sell out earliest.
- Compare nonstop and one-stop options. A one-stop itinerary can be much cheaper, but it adds travel time and the risk of a missed connection.
- Book connecting flights on a single ticket where possible. If a first flight is delayed, the airline is then responsible for rebooking you onto the next flight.

## Connections
- Minimum connection times vary by airport. As a rule of thumb, allow at least 60 minutes for domestic connections and 90–120 minutes for international connections, especially when you must clear immigration or change terminals.
- When entering the United States, you clear immigration and customs at your first US airport, even if you are connecting onward.

## Check-in and airport time
- Arrive about 2 hours before short-haul international flights and about 3 hours before long-haul international flights.
- Check the airline's baggage allowance, especially on low-cost carriers, which often charge for cabin bags and checked bags.

## Flight time and jet lag
- Eastbound flights across many time zones usually cause stronger jet lag than westbound flights.
- To adjust faster, get daylight in the morning at your destination, stay hydrated, and avoid long naps on the first day.
- Scheduled flight times include taxiing, so the published duration is usually longer than the time in the air.
