# Travel insurance exclusions and claims

This guide describes common exclusions and claim steps. Always check the exact wording of your own policy.

## Common exclusions
- Pre-existing medical conditions, unless declared and accepted or covered by a waiver.
- Injuries from high-risk activities that are not added to the policy.
- Incidents involving alcohol or drug intoxication.
- Events that were already known when you bought the policy, such as a storm that had already been named or a strike that had already been announced.
- Travelling to a country or region against official government travel advice.
- Unattended valuables, and cash above a small limit.
- Changing your mind, or not having the right visa or passport validity.

## Making a claim
1. Contact the insurer's emergency assistance line before receiving non-urgent treatment abroad, if you can. Some insurers will not pay for care they did not approve in advance.
2. Keep all receipts, medical reports, booking confirmations and proof of payment.
3. Report theft to the local police within 24 hours and get a written report.
4. For lost or delayed bags, file a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) with the airline before leaving the airport.
5. For flight delays or cancellations, get written confirmation of the delay and its cause from the airline.
6. Submit the claim within the policy deadline, often 30–90 days after you return.

## Tips for choosing a policy
- Compare the medical and evacuation limits, not just the price.
- Check the deductible (excess) for each benefit.
- Make sure every traveller and every destination is named correctly.
- Declare any medical conditions honestly; an undeclared condition can invalidate a claim.
