# Travel insurance basics

This guide gives general information about travel insurance. It is not advice about a specific policy. Coverage, limits and exclusions vary between insurers and countries, so always read the policy wording before buying.

## What travel insurance does
Travel insurance reimburses or pays for losses that happen before or during a trip. The most common benefits are trip cancellation, emergency medical treatment abroad, medical evacuation, lost or delayed baggage, and travel delays.

## When to buy
Buy soon after you make your first trip payment. Cancellation cover only protects you against events that happen after the policy starts. Some benefits, such as waivers for pre-existing medical conditions or "Cancel For Any Reason" upgrades, are often only available if you buy within a short window (commonly 10–21 days) of your first trip deposit.

## Single-trip versus annual policies
- Single-trip policies cover one journey and are usually priced from the trip cost, length and destination.
- Annual multi-trip policies cover many trips in a year, usually with a maximum length per trip (for example 30, 45 or 60 days). They are often cheaper for people who travel three or more times a year.

## How premiums are usually priced
Comprehensive single-trip policies commonly cost around 4–10% of the insured trip cost. The main pricing factors are:
- Traveller age: premiums rise with age, especially from about 55–60 and again from about 70.
- Trip cost: higher prepaid, non-refundable costs mean higher cancellation exposure.
- Trip length: longer trips increase the medical and delay risk.
- Destination: trips to countries with expensive healthcare, such as the United States, usually cost more to insure.
- Coverage level and deductible: higher limits and lower deductibles increase the premium.

## Check your existing coverage first
Some credit cards include travel insurance if you pay for the trip with the card. Your home health insurance may not cover you abroad. For example, US Medicare generally does not cover care outside the United States. UK GHIC and EU EHIC cards give access to state healthcare in some countries, but they do not replace travel insurance or cover repatriation.
