# Travel insurance coverage types

This guide explains common types of travel insurance cover. Exact definitions and limits depend on each policy.

## Trip cancellation
Reimburses prepaid, non-refundable trip costs if you cancel for a covered reason before departure. Covered reasons usually include serious illness or injury of the traveller or a close family member, death in the family, jury duty, and some severe weather or natural disasters. Changing your mind is not a covered reason.

## Trip interruption
Covers the unused part of the trip and extra travel costs if you have to cut a trip short or return home for a covered reason.

## Emergency medical
Pays for emergency treatment abroad. For international trips, many consumer guides suggest at least USD 50,000–100,000 of medical cover, and more for destinations with very expensive healthcare such as the United States.

## Medical evacuation and repatriation
Pays to move you to a suitable hospital or back home when medically necessary. This can cost tens of thousands of dollars, so limits of USD 100,000–250,000 or more are commonly recommended, especially for remote destinations or cruises.

## Baggage loss and delay
Covers lost, stolen or damaged luggage up to a limit, usually with per-item limits for valuables. Baggage delay cover pays for essential items if checked bags are delayed beyond a set number of hours.

## Travel delay and missed connections
Pays for meals and accommodation when a flight or other transport is delayed beyond a set time, and may cover extra costs to catch up with your itinerary if you miss a connection.

## Cancel For Any Reason (CFAR)
An optional upgrade that lets you cancel for reasons not otherwise covered. It usually reimburses only 50–75% of the trip cost, must be bought soon after the first trip deposit, and requires cancelling a set time (often 48 hours or more) before departure.

## Rental car damage
Covers damage to a rental car. Check whether your credit card or the rental agreement already provides this.

## Adventure and sports cover
Activities such as skiing, scuba diving or mountaineering are often excluded from standard policies and need an add-on.
