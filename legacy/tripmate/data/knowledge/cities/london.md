# London travel guide

## Airports and getting into the city
London has five main airports. Heathrow (LHR) is the largest and handles most long-haul flights. Gatwick (LGW) is the second busiest, with many European and leisure routes. Stansted (STN) and Luton (LTN) mostly serve low-cost European carriers. London City (LCY) is small, close to Canary Wharf, and popular for short business trips.

From Heathrow, the Elizabeth line runs directly into central London (about 30–40 minutes to the West End). The Heathrow Express to Paddington is faster (about 15 minutes) but costs more. The Piccadilly line on the Underground is the cheapest option and the slowest. From Gatwick, the Gatwick Express and Thameslink trains reach central London in about 30–35 minutes.

## Getting around
The Underground ("the Tube"), buses, Overground, Elizabeth line and DLR all accept contactless bank cards and phone payments. Pay-as-you-go fares are capped daily and weekly, so you do not need to buy a travelcard in advance. Buses do not accept cash. Central London is walkable, and many sights along the Thames are close together.

## When to visit
Late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September) usually offer mild weather and longer days. Summer is the busiest and most expensive season. Rain can happen in any month, so pack a light waterproof layer. Winter is cold and dark early, but hotel prices drop outside the Christmas period.

## Neighbourhoods and sights
- Westminster: Houses of Parliament, Big Ben, Westminster Abbey, Buckingham Palace.
- South Bank: the London Eye, Tate Modern, Borough Market, riverside walks.
- Covent Garden and the West End: theatres, shopping, restaurants.
- South Kensington: the Natural History Museum, Science Museum and V&A, all free to enter.
- Shoreditch and East London: street art, markets, nightlife.
- Bloomsbury: the British Museum (free entry).

## Practical information
- Currency: pound sterling (GBP). Cards are accepted almost everywhere.
- Power: type G plugs, 230V.
- Tipping: restaurants often add a 10–12.5% service charge; check the bill before tipping extra.
- Entry: many visitors who do not need a visa now need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) before travelling to the UK. Check the current rules on gov.uk before booking.
- Emergency number: 999 (or 112).

## Safety
London is generally safe for visitors. Watch for pickpockets on crowded Tube trains, at busy tourist sites and on Oxford Street. Use licensed black cabs or app-based rides rather than unbooked minicabs.
