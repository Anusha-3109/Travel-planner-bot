# New York City travel guide

## Airports and getting into the city
Three major airports serve New York. John F. Kennedy (JFK) in Queens handles most long-haul international flights. LaGuardia (LGA), also in Queens, mainly serves domestic flights. Newark Liberty (EWR) is in New Jersey and has many international and domestic flights.

From JFK, the AirTrain connects to the subway (A and E lines) and to the Long Island Rail Road at Jamaica, which reaches Penn Station or Grand Central in about 35–60 minutes in total. From Newark, the AirTrain connects to NJ Transit and Amtrak trains to Penn Station (about 30 minutes). LaGuardia is served by buses such as the Q70 express to the subway, and by taxis.

## Getting around
The subway runs 24 hours a day. You can pay with contactless bank cards or phones through the OMNY system, and weekly fares are capped. Manhattan is easy to walk, especially north–south on the avenues. Yellow cabs and app-based rides are widely available, but traffic can be slow.

## When to visit
Spring (April–June) and autumn (September–November) have the most comfortable weather. Summer is hot and humid. Winter can be very cold with occasional snow, and the December holiday period is busy and expensive.

## Neighbourhoods and sights
- Midtown: Times Square, Rockefeller Center, the Empire State Building, Broadway theatres.
- Central Park and the Upper East Side: the Metropolitan Museum of Art and Museum Mile.
- Lower Manhattan: the 9/11 Memorial and Museum, Wall Street, ferries to the Statue of Liberty.
- Greenwich Village and SoHo: historic streets, shops, restaurants.
- Brooklyn: DUMBO, Brooklyn Bridge Park, Williamsburg.
- Upper West Side: the American Museum of Natural History.

## Practical information
- Currency: US dollar (USD). Cards are accepted almost everywhere.
- Power: type A and B plugs, 120V.
- Tipping: about 18–20% at sit-down restaurants, and a few dollars per drink at bars.
- Entry: travellers from Visa Waiver Program countries need an approved ESTA before flying. Others need a visa. Check the US Department of State and CBP websites.
- Emergency number: 911.

## Safety
New York is generally safe in tourist areas. Stay aware on late-night subway rides, keep valuables out of sight in crowds, and use official taxi ranks at airports.
