# Tokyo travel guide

## Airports and getting into the city
Tokyo has two international airports. Haneda (HND) is close to the city, about 20–30 minutes from central areas by Keikyu train or Tokyo Monorail. Narita (NRT) is about 60–90 minutes away; the Narita Express (N'EX) and the Keisei Skyliner are the fastest rail links, and limousine buses serve major hotels.

## Getting around
Tokyo's rail and subway network is extensive, punctual and clean. Rechargeable IC cards (Suica or Pasmo, including phone versions) work on almost all trains, subways and buses, and in many convenience stores. Avoid rush hour (about 7:30–9:30 and 17:30–19:30) with luggage. Trains generally stop running around midnight.

## When to visit
Spring (late March to early April) is cherry blossom season and is very popular, so book early. Autumn (October–November) has comfortable temperatures and autumn colours. Summer (July–August) is hot and humid, and typhoons are possible from about August to October. Winter is cool, dry and usually sunny.

## Neighbourhoods and sights
- Shinjuku: skyscrapers, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government observation decks (free), Omoide Yokocho, Shinjuku Gyoen garden.
- Shibuya: the famous scramble crossing, shopping, Shibuya Sky viewpoint.
- Harajuku and Omotesando: youth fashion on Takeshita Street, Meiji Jingu shrine.
- Asakusa: Senso-ji temple and Nakamise shopping street.
- Ginza: department stores and upscale dining.
- Akihabara: electronics, anime and gaming culture.

## Practical information
- Currency: Japanese yen (JPY). Cards are widely accepted in cities, but carry some cash for small shops, shrines and rural areas.
- Power: type A plugs, 100V. Check that your devices support 100V.
- Tipping: not customary and can cause confusion.
- Entry: many nationalities can enter visa-free for short stays. Visit Japan Web lets you complete immigration and customs forms before arrival. Check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan website for current rules.
- Emergency numbers: 110 (police), 119 (ambulance and fire).

## Etiquette and safety
Tokyo has very low crime. Keep phone calls off on trains, queue in marked lines on platforms, and do not eat while walking in busy areas. Many restaurants and shops are cashless or cash-only, so check before ordering.
