# Paris travel guide

## Airports and getting into the city
Paris has two main airports. Charles de Gaulle (CDG), north-east of the city, handles most international and long-haul flights. Orly (ORY), to the south, serves many European, domestic and North African routes. Beauvais (BVA) is about 85 km away and is used by some low-cost airlines; plan for a long coach transfer.

From CDG, the RER B train reaches central Paris (Gare du Nord, Châtelet) in about 35–50 minutes. Taxis from CDG and Orly to central Paris charge fixed fares that depend on whether you are going to the Right Bank or Left Bank. Metro line 14 now connects Orly directly with central Paris.

## Getting around
The Metro is dense and frequent, and most sights are within a short walk of a station. The RER suburban trains are faster for longer distances. Tickets can be loaded onto a Navigo Easy card or bought in the official transport app. Paris is very walkable, and the Vélib' bike-share system is useful in good weather.

## When to visit
April–June and September–October usually have pleasant weather. July and August are busy with tourists, and many local businesses close for part of August. Winter is cold and grey but quieter, with shorter queues at museums.

## Neighbourhoods and sights
- Le Marais: historic streets, boutiques, the Picasso Museum, falafel on Rue des Rosiers.
- Saint-Germain-des-Prés and the Latin Quarter: cafés, bookshops, the Luxembourg Gardens.
- Montmartre: Sacré-Cœur, artists' square, views over the city.
- Louvre and Tuileries: the Louvre Museum (closed Tuesdays; book a timed entry slot).
- Musée d'Orsay: Impressionist art (closed Mondays).
- Eiffel Tower and Champ de Mars: book summit tickets in advance in peak season.

## Practical information
- Currency: euro (EUR). Cards are widely accepted.
- Power: type C and E plugs, 230V.
- Tipping: service is included in restaurant prices ("service compris"); rounding up or leaving small change is optional.
- Entry: France is in the Schengen Area. The EU Entry/Exit System registers many non-EU visitors at the border, and the ETIAS travel authorisation is planned. Check the official EU travel pages before booking.
- Emergency number: 112.

## Safety
Paris is generally safe, but pickpocketing is common on the Metro (especially line 1), around the Eiffel Tower, at Sacré-Cœur and in the Louvre. Ignore petition and "friendship bracelet" approaches. Keep bags closed and in front of you in crowds.
